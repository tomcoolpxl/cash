## Description

`7z l ARCHIVE` lists an archive, `7z t` tests it, `7z x` extracts it with its folders and
`7z e` without them, into the current folder or `-oFOLDER`. Names after the archive
choose items, `-x!NAME` leaves some out, `-r` looks for them in every folder. `-so`
writes items to standard output, `-slt` lists every property, `-ba` leaves the headers
out.

`7z a ARCHIVE NAMES` adds files and folders to an archive, making it if need be (`.7z`
is added to a name without an extension); `7z u` adds only what is new or changed,
`7z d` deletes items, `7z rn ARCHIVE OLD NEW` renames them, a folder with what is in it.
`-u` changes what happens to each kind of item, `-u!OTHER.7z` writes a second archive.
`-mx0` to `-mx9` choose the level, `-m0=METHOD` the method (LZMA2 unless named; LZMA,
PPMd, BZip2, Deflate, Copy), `-ms` the solid blocks, `-mf` the filter (by default BCJ
and its kin for executables and Delta for WAV, as 7-Zip chooses), `-mhe` and `-p` the
encryption, `-mhc`, `-mtm`, `-mtc`, `-mta` and `-mtr` what the header keeps. `-sdel`
deletes what was added, `-stl` gives the archive its newest item's time, `-w` says where
the new archive is written before it replaces the old one.

The command line, messages, listings and exit codes are 7-Zip 26.03's: switches
anywhere on the line, `-i` and `-x` with their `r`, `w` and `m`, `@listfile`, the
overwrite question and `-ao`, `-y`, `-p`, `-bb`, `-bs` and `-ssc`. A wildcard in the
archive's name names every archive it matches.

It is all in Rust, in cash: 7z archives with LZMA, LZMA2, PPMd, bzip2, Deflate,
Deflate64 and Copy, the BCJ, BCJ2, ARM64 and Delta filters, solid blocks, and 7-Zip's
AES, with the headers encrypted or not.

## Windows notes

- Lines end in LF and names show `/`, where 7-Zip on Windows writes CRLF and `\`.
- Times are shown in the zone the shell's `TZ` names, else in Windows' own.
- Switches start with `-`, as in 7-Zip itself.
- Compressed archives hold the same items as 7-Zip's, but not its bytes: the encoders
  are other ones. Stored archives (`-mx0 -mhc=off`) are 7-Zip's to the byte. x86
  executables get BCJ where 7-Zip's `-mx8` and `-mx9` use BCJ2.
- A 7-Zip installed by Scoop or by its installer stays reachable by its path, or after
  `enable -n 7z`.

## Examples

```
7z l archive.7z
7z x archive.7z -oout            # with its folders, into out
7z e -so archive.7z notes.txt | less
7z t -psecret secret.7z
7z x '*.7z' -y                   # every archive here, replacing without asking
7z a backup.7z docs -mx9         # docs and all in it, at the highest level
7z u backup.7z docs              # only what changed since
7z a -p -mhe=on secret.7z notes  # asks for the password; names hidden too
7z d backup.7z 'docs/*.tmp' -r
```
